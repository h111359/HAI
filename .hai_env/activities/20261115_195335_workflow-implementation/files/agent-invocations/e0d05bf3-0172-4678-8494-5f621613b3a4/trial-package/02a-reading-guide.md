# Plain-language reading guide

This explains specification Draft 1, in the same order. The specification holds the authoritative proposal; reading this guide does not approve it. A custodian looks after records. A pending request is not a confirmed booking. An incident is a report of damage, loss or missing parts.

## 1. People and responsibilities

[Specification section](02-specification.md#1-people-and-responsibilities)

The organizer would choose policy; the two volunteers would handle requests and handovers. Naming one volunteer per request helps prevent it getting lost between them. For example, Maya handles a drill booking and Sam takes over using the same record. These roles are proposed. The organizer's authority and who can assess equipment still need D06 review.

## 2. Items and eligibility

[Specification section](02-specification.md#2-items-and-eligibility)

Each of the 20 items would have a label and condition entry to avoid confusing similar equipment. ITEM-07 could mean one particular drill and charger. The 20-item scale is established; the labels are a proposed recording method. D01 must decide who can borrow and how eligibility is checked. Age, neighborhood boundary and group borrowing are not decided. Venue and equipment readiness also remain open.

## 3. Reservations and changes

[Specification section](02-specification.md#3-reservations-and-changes)

A volunteer would confirm an available item and two Saturdays before making a promise. One shared register and one editor at a time help avoid double bookings: Sam sees Maya's booking before answering a competing request. This recording method is proposed. D02 must choose fair allocation, contact route, limits and no-show handling. Extensions need a new confirmation; late returns can disrupt the next booking. If the register is inaccessible, new confirmations pause while commitments are reconciled; an item received still gets a temporary receipt.

## 4. Collection, return and interruptions

[Specification section](02-specification.md#4-collection-return-and-interruptions)

The borrower and volunteer would compare condition and accessories at both handovers. For example, both note an existing scratch on the drill case. This proposed record helps separate old damage from new concerns; it does not decide liability. Saturday-only handovers are required by the brief. One-week loans, hours and venue remain D03 proposals. Missed returns or cancelled sessions move to a later Saturday; questions can still be sent on another day. No unattended drop-off is assumed.

## 5. Damage, loss and disputes

[Specification section](02-specification.md#5-damage-loss-and-disputes)

A volunteer would record what happened and hold back affected equipment; the organizer would hear the borrower's account before deciding. For example, a missing charger makes the drill unavailable and the next borrower is informed. These are proposals. D04 must settle wear, responsibility, valuation, fees and reconsideration. No charge is automatic; online payments are excluded by the brief, while offline charges remain undecided. An item can be physically returned while a damage case stays open.

## 6. Records and ownership

[Specification section](02-specification.md#6-records-and-ownership)

The group needs someone answerable for personal information and someone to keep records accessible to replacements. Sam should find Alex's return date and pending incident when Maya is away; another borrower should not see the register. Group accountability, custodian and backup are recommendations. D05 must name the actual owner and decide storage, access, retention, correction/deletion, backup and departure arrangements. Paper is harder to share; a shared file needs account access. Item ownership does not settle personal information ownership, and this trial folder is not the proposed service store.
